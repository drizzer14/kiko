Privacy Policy for Kiko

Effective date: 9 November 2026

Kiko is a personal finance app developed by Dmytro Vasylkivskyi ("we", "us"). This policy explains how Kiko handles your information.

Short version: Your data stays on your device. Kiko has no server of ours, and we never receive, store, or have access to your financial data.

Kiko is an independent app. It is not a bank or a financial institution, and it is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or partnered with any bank, broker, or other service named in the app. Bank and broker names appear only to label the accounts and statements you choose to add. Kiko never initiates payments or moves money — it only displays data you provide.

1. We operate no server and collect nothing

Kiko has no backend operated by us. There is no Kiko account to create. We do not collect, receive, transmit to ourselves, or have any access to your data. Everything you do in Kiko happens on your own device.

2. Data stored on your device

The information you enter or sync is stored only on your device:

  • Your accounts, holdings, transactions, balances, prices, categories, transaction templates, category rules, and settings.
  • The API credentials you choose to add (for example a Monobank token or a Binance API key and secret).
  • The reminders you set up. iOS shows them as local notifications on your device; their text never names an account or an amount.
  • Import progress from a connected service (read positions, times, and market symbols), so that a sync continues where it stopped.

This data is kept in an on-device database encrypted with AES-256 (SQLCipher). Your API credentials are stored in the iOS Keychain, the system's secure credential store.

Exports. When you export your data, Kiko writes an encrypted file (AES-256-GCM) with a key that is shown only to you. The file includes your saved tokens and keys only if you turn on "Include saved passwords & keys" (off by default) and confirm with Face ID or your device passcode. Kiko never uploads an export anywhere; you choose where to save or share it. Keep the file and its key safe.

3. Connections to third-party services

Kiko communicates directly from your device with the services below, only as far as your accounts and holdings need them. Some connections run only after you connect an account, add a Bitcoin address, or save a key (Monobank statements, Binance account data, Bybit account data, Blockstream). Others run automatically to value what you track: currency exchange rates (Monobank and the National Bank of Ukraine), cryptocurrency prices (CoinGecko, and Binance public prices when CoinGecko has none), and precious-metal prices and the government bond register (National Bank of Ukraine). Kiko does not route these connections through any server of ours. Like any internet connection, each service can see your device's IP address.

  • Monobank (api.monobank.ua) — your bank account information and statements, using your personal token; also public currency exchange rates, for which no token or personal data is sent.
  • Binance (api.binance.com) — using your read-only API key: your balances of every coin and fiat currency (spot, funding, Simple Earn); your deposit and withdrawal history; your Spot trade history; your Convert history; your conversions of small balances to BNB (dust); your P2P orders (the amounts and fiat totals of your purchases; no data about the other party); your fiat deposits and withdrawals and your card purchases and sales; your Binance Pay history, with the Binance Pay name of the other party as the description; your transfers between your Binance wallets (universal transfer); and your Simple Earn subscriptions, redemptions and rewards. For a connected account, Kiko also reads Binance's public list of markets, which needs no key. On your device, in the import progress, Kiko keeps the tickers of coins it does not track and, for a record it does not import yet (for example a P2P sale), only the record's kind, order number or id, and time; this also travels in a .kiko export. Also, for any user, public market prices of a coin CoinGecko did not price: Kiko sends only the market symbol (for example BTCUSDT) and the dates of the prices it needs; no key, address, or amount is sent.
  • Bybit (api.bybit.com): using your read-only API key: details of the key itself (whether it is read-only, whether it belongs to your main account, and when it ends); your balances of every coin and fiat currency (Unified, Funding); your deposit history, including deposits sent by another Bybit user, your withdrawal history; your transfers between your Bybit wallets; your Unified account's transaction log, from which Kiko imports spot trades; your convert history; and your Funding wallet's transaction record. Bybit's answers can include your Bybit user ID and the sender's or recipient's address, email, phone number or Bybit user ID. They reach only your device, and Kiko does not keep those details, with one exception named at the end of this entry. Kiko keeps on your device the tickers of coins it does not track or could not read, the wallets it left out, the kinds of log entries it did not import, the date your key ends, and, inside each imported trade or convert and each convert it could not import, Bybit's IDs of that record and its order, which can hold your account number; for each move of your Funding wallet's record and each move matched to one, its coin, direction, amount, fee, tax, time, kind of move and Bybit's ID, also for one that Kiko does not list; this also travels in a .kiko export. The exception: from your own Bybit user ID, Kiko makes a value and keeps it on your device and in a .kiko export, so that it can refuse a key of another Bybit account while an account in Kiko still has holdings from the first. The value is not the ID itself, but the ID is a short number: anyone who can read the value, for example from a .kiko export and its key, can find your ID with a script. A later read of a key replaces the value, and deleting that account in Kiko deletes it.
  • Blockstream (blockstream.info) — the public on-chain balance of a Bitcoin address you add. Kiko sends that address to Blockstream to look it up.
  • CoinGecko (api.coingecko.com) — the current price and the daily price history of each cryptocurrency you hold. Kiko sends only the names of those coins (for example bitcoin); no address, amount, or credential is sent.
  • National Bank of Ukraine (bank.gov.ua) — public currency exchange rates and their history, precious-metal prices, and the public register of government bonds. Kiko downloads the whole register, so the ISIN you enter is not sent. No personal data is sent.

Each service processes your requests under its own privacy policy. Kiko sends only what a service needs to return your data. Connections that carry a credential use TLS with certificate pinning.

4. No analytics, tracking, or advertising

Kiko contains no analytics, advertising, tracking, or crash-reporting software. We do not track you across apps or websites, and we do not build a profile of you. Kiko does not display ads and does not sell or share your data.

5. Security

  • The on-device database is encrypted with AES-256 (SQLCipher).
  • API credentials are stored in the iOS Keychain.
  • Credential-bearing network connections use TLS with certificate pinning.
  • You can protect the app with Face ID or your device passcode.

6. Data retention and deletion

Your data lives only on your device. You can delete accounts, holdings, and transactions inside the app at any time. Deleting the app removes its database from your device, but iOS can keep items saved in the Keychain, such as your Monobank token and Binance keys, after the app is deleted. To remove them, first disconnect each connected account (open the account and tap Disconnect), then delete the app.

Deleted statement rows. When you delete a transaction that came from an imported statement, Kiko keeps a key of that row and the time you deleted it, on your device and in a .kiko export, so that a new import of the same statement recognizes the row. The key holds the row's id from the statement or, if it has no id, the row's time, amount, and description. Kiko removes the key when the row is imported again, when the holding gets a new balance, or when the holding is deleted.

7. Children

Kiko is not directed to children under 13 and does not knowingly collect data from them.
